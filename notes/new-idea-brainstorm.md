# New Idea Brainstorm: Contract Selling in Provo

*From a brainstorm with Claude, Oct 29, 2026, ahead of build night with Caleb and Thomas.*

## Why we're moving on from Rheo

- Ava's read: "the problem isn't real." No one was interested when we reached out (1 reply from 34 emails, and it was a decline).
- Our own simulation backs it up: only $10–94 in fuel savings per vehicle per month, and no earlier deliveries.
- **Lesson to carry forward:** every version of Rheo started from the technology and went looking for a problem. This time, start from a problem people already pay to deal with, and test it by trying to sell before building anything.

## Ideas we brainstormed

Each one is framed as a problem someone already pays to solve:

- Admin for small-business owners (quotes, invoices, scheduling, follow-ups)
- **Student housing and subleasing in Provo** ← picked to explore
- Inquiries, quotes and deposits for event vendors
- Matching tutors and test-prep help
- Photos, listings, messages and shipping for local creators and Etsy sellers
- No-shows, reminders and intake paperwork at clinics and small offices
- Recruiting for student clubs and startups

## The idea: contract selling

**The problem.** In Provo, subleasing usually means selling your housing contract. Students leave for internships, missions, marriage or graduation and keep paying rent until they find a replacement.

**Whose problem is worst:** the student who's selling. They have a deadline and lose money every month the room sits empty. (Rough guess: $400–600 a month. Check this with real sellers.)

**What people use today:** mostly Facebook housing groups and KSL Classifieds, both free.

**Watch out for:**

- "Zillow for subleasing" is a solution, not a problem. That's the Rheo trap.
- A new listing site has to beat free options.
- A marketplace needs buyers and sellers at the same time to work.

## The test: sell the service before building the app

Offer sellers a **done-for-you contract-selling service**: we write the listing, post it everywhere and screen the replies. They pay a fee only if the contract sells, like a real estate agent for housing contracts.

- **Who:** 5 named students trying to sell a contract right now, reached in person or by text
- **The ask:** agree to the success fee (an amount they'd actually pay)
- **Pass mark:** set it before talking to anyone, e.g. 2 of 5 say yes
- **Deadline:** Thu, Nov 5
- **No building** until the test passes

If no one takes the deal, an app wouldn't have changed their minds either.
